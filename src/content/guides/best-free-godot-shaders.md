Shaders are the fastest way to make a Godot game look dramatically better, and the library is full of free ones. The hard part is choosing well — this guide covers where to look, what separates a great shader from a mediocre one, and how to use what you install.

## Where to find free shaders

The [Shaders category](/category/shaders) is the dedicated home for shader assets. Two other categories are worth checking too:

- [Materials](/category/materials) — ready-made 3D surface materials that often bundle shader logic.
- [2D Tools](/category/2d%20tools) and [3D Tools](/category/3d%20tools) — tools that frequently ship shader-based effects as part of a plugin.

Filter the [Shaders category](/category/shaders) by [Godot 4](/engine/4.0) or [Godot 3](/engine/3.0) to match your engine version, then sort by **Highest rated** to see what other developers actually use.

## What makes a shader worth installing

1. **Preview images that show the effect** — a shader is visual; if there is no preview, you are buying blind.
2. **Documented parameters** — the best shaders expose colors, speeds and strengths in the inspector and explain them in the README.
3. **Version compatibility** — shader syntax changed between Godot 3 and Godot 4. Check the engine badge before installing.
4. **Recent updates** — a shader updated within the last year is more likely to work on current engine versions.
5. **Positive reviews** — check the approval percentage and read the text reviews for real-world notes.

## Types of shaders worth grabbing

- **Post-processing packs** — color grading, retro effects, CRT and VHS looks applied scene-wide with a single node.
- **Water and nature systems** — animated water, grass, fire and weather effects with tunable parameters.
- **UI and outline shaders** — hover effects, animated borders and character outlines that add polish with minimal effort.
- **Dissolve and transition shaders** — handy for cutscenes and level transitions.

## How to use them

Once installed, attach the shader as a `ShaderMaterial` on the node you want to affect and tune the parameters in the inspector. The [shader guide](/guides/how-to-use-shaders-in-godot) walks through this step by step.

## Judging quality honestly

Be wary of shaders with zero reviews and no previews — a great thumbnail can hide a shader that only works in one specific project setup. Prefer assets with a live repository (follow the repository link on the asset page) and a README that documents requirements. For a deeper checklist, read [how to choose a Godot asset](/guides/how-to-choose-a-godot-asset).
